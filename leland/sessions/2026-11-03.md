## Round 6: the first real training run, and "why no Mac version?"
- 750 rounds of Pollen's training on the PC: 43 min 28 s, and **the duck walks** (TRAINING.md section 12).
- **Q: why isn't Pollen's training rewritten for the Mac?** Not because of Unix. Their speed comes from running
  thousands of ducks on the graphics card, and the physics engine (MuJoCo Warp) is written in NVIDIA's Warp/CUDA.
  The neural-network part (PyTorch) already runs on Apple's GPU; the physics is the hard part.
- **Q: how hard would a Metal rewrite be?** Not impossible, but a big project. Ways it could go, hardest last:
  1. **MJX on Apple's `jax-metal`** (Google's GPU MuJoCo, built on JAX): exists today, experimental, missing
     pieces. Pollen's environment (rewards, curriculum, randomisation) would still need re-writing on top.
  2. **A Metal backend for Warp** (Warp is open source and already generates C++/CUDA code): generate Metal code
     instead. Hard parts: the GPU features MuJoCo Warp leans on for speed (CUDA graphs, atomics, tile operations),
     and keeping up as NVIDIA changes Warp. NVIDIA is unlikely to maintain it.
  3. **Hand-port MuJoCo Warp's kernels to Metal:** tens of thousands of lines, moving every week.
  - Rough size: a working prototype for just the Microduck is months of expert GPU work; a maintained port is ongoing
    work forever. On paper the Studio's GPU is in the same league as the 3060, but that's unmeasured.
  - Practical answer for me: the PC already trains a walker in 46 minutes, so a port would be a hobby or learning
    project, not a need. A small first experiment: try MJX + `jax-metal` on the Studio.

## Round 7: "you do the work, I'd be the open-source contributor": does that change things?
- Searched first: **someone has already started.** David Dobas / innate-inc built **`warp-metal`** (Apache-2.0,
  Sept 2026): it adds an Apple GPU (`metal:0`) to NVIDIA's Warp. With two small patches to MuJoCo Warp and mjlab
  (open PRs on his forks) it runs mjlab, and **his fork `DavidDobas/microduck_rl`, branch `metal`, trains the
  Microduck on a Mac** (8 commits, 4 files changed, last 2026-09-20; it's 30 commits behind Pollen's `develop`).
  Training uses PyTorch on Apple's GPU (`MJLAB_AGENT_DEVICE=mps`).
- His benchmark: Unitree G1, 1,024 environments, about **1.0 s per iteration on an M5 Pro vs 0.3 s on an RTX 5090**, with
  the gap widening as environments increase (Apple GPU threadgroup memory). Limits: no float64, some atomics are
  not truly atomic, Apple's compiler may miscompile big unrolled matrix maths.
- Barely noticed so far (1 star, 0 forks). Other attempts: MJX on `jax-metal` (broken: `mhlo.reduce`), two
  MLX ports (narrow feature sets).

## Round 9: "the duck already walks, so why train walking?"
- Yes, it's the **"hello world"**: a skill with a known good answer (Pollen's `alpha_walking`), so it proves the whole
  pipeline (PC, Studio, Metal, our tools, the walking test) before spending hours on something new.
- Other reasons to train walking anyway: a custom gait or style, more robustness (rough ground, slopes, pushes), my own
  unit's servos once it arrives, and learning the craft. New skills often build on it, too.
- **What else Pollen's repo can train** (task names in `microduck_rl`): standing up (`StandUp`), sit/stand
  (`SitStand`), forward roll (`Roulade`), spin (`Spin`), ball kick (`BallKick`), picking up from the ground
  (`GroundPick`), roller-skate skills (`RollerCrouch`, `RollerSlope`, `RollerStandUp`), stand at speed (`VelStand`),
  plus **Rough** (uneven ground) and **Backlash** (models the gears' play, closer to the real robot) variants.
- Official policies shipped with the duck (`~/.cache/duck-sim/policies`): alpha_walking, alpha_stand,
  alpha_sitstand, alpha_ground_pick, ball_kick_left/right, roller, roller_crouch, roulade, velstand.

## Round 10: what should the next goal be? (advice, waiting for my decision)
- The duck arrives with Pollen's official policies, and they're updated by releases, so a working duck on day one is
  already covered. Our goal: **be able to train, check and trust any skill on demand**, not a stockpile of policies.
- Why not train everything now and upload it on arrival: Pollen's code changes daily, so a policy trained months
  early may not fit the robot software it ships with. An untested policy can make a real duck fall. Retrain near
  arrival on Pollen's latest code; keep the skills and the checking routine ready.
- Each skill is its **own separate policy** (the robot switches between them), so skills are trained separately, one
  at a time, two at once when using both the PC and the Studio.
- Proposed phases: (1) tonight, walking 750 → 4,000 on the PC (quiet 130 W cap, auto-off) + first new skill
  `StandUp` on the Studio; (2) a "report card" routine: walking test, video, then the full duck simulator; (3) the
  other skills one by one, short run first, long run only if it's learning; (4) near arrival: retrain on Pollen's
  latest code with the Backlash (real-gear) variants; on the real duck, official policies first, then ours one at a
  time with the duck held.
