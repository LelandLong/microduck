A follow-up, and a bug that I don't think is Metal's fault.

Stand-up on Metal. I trained Mjlab-StandUp-Flat-MicroDuck for 750 iterations on the M4 Max (48 min, about 3.8 s/iteration). When I filmed it, the ducks that failed to stand had sunk through the floor. I counted the robot geoms that can collide with the terrain in the compiled model:

Model Pollen's develop (mjlab 1.3.0) your metal branch (mjlab 1.6.0)
robot_walk.xml (velocity) 2 (feet) 2 (feet)
robot_groundcontact.xml (standup, sitstand, ground pick, roulade, rollers) 10 (trunk, hips, legs, jaw, feet) 2 (feet only)

The cause seems to be the mjlab upgrade, not warp-metal. The body-contact geoms in robot_groundcontact.xml have no names. Both versions apply FULL_COLLISION (.*_collision) with disable_other_geoms=True, but:

  • mjlab 1.3 disabled the non-matching geoms by name, so the unnamed ones accidentally kept their contacts;
  • mjlab 1.6 iterates over the geom objects, so they're now (correctly) disabled.

Your contype=1, conaffinity=1 change keeps the named feet colliding, but not the unnamed body parts.

Velocity/walking is unaffected, and I confirmed my earlier walking result stands. Possible fixes: name those geoms so they match .*_collision, or set disable_other_geoms=False on the ground-contact robot's CollisionCfg. I'm happy to open a PR for whichever you prefer.

Walking, longer run, for reference: on the RTX 3060 I resumed the 750-iteration run to 4,000 (about 3 h 10 min more). It now walks straight; the 750-iteration policy curved about 70° over 8 s, which I'd missed by only measuring forward distance.
