Kevin Wood: Microduck, getting started training a walking policy with mjlab

Video (8:20). Watched 2026-10-30. These are my notes, not the transcript; timestamps link into the video.

The steps

  1. Clone pollen-robotics/microduck_rl, the training repo, not microduck (0:35).
  2. uv run train Mjlab-Velocity-Flat-MicroDuck --env.scene.num-envs 4096
  3. On native Windows this fails with "selected GPUs index out of range" (1:15). Fix: use WSL2. In PowerShell run wsl --install, pick an Ubuntu version, then repeat steps 1–2 inside WSL (1:39).
  4. The first uv run builds two packages: microduck itself and BAM (Better Actuator Models).
  5. It then offers a Weights & Biases account for charts. Option 3 = skip (4:53).
  6. Training starts: 50,000 iterations by default, about 3.8 s each on his machine (5:35).
  7. A checkpoint (model_N.pt) is saved every 250 iterations under logs/rsl_rl/.... You can watch one while training continues (6:50): uv run play <task> --checkpoint-file logs/rsl_rl/<task>/<date>/model_750.pt

Things worth remembering

  • You don't need 50,000 iterations. It walks after 750 (~48 min at his speed) and walks with clear purpose after 4,000 (~4 h). The full default would be ~53 h. Change it with --agent.max_iterations; in the code it's written 50_000, Python's way of writing 50,000.
  • The sim-to-real gap is the difference between how the duck behaves in simulation and in real life. Pollen narrows it with system identification: they measured the real Dynamixel servos and built a matching model (BAM). Friction is usually what separates a policy that works on the real robot from one that doesn't.
  • mjlab is the training framework, an Isaac Lab-style framework on MuJoCo Warp (GPU-accelerated MuJoCo), which is why training needs an NVIDIA GPU.

What it means for my setup

  • Confirms the plan for my gaming PC (RTX 3060): WSL2, not native Windows.
  • Cost check for cloud training: 4,000 iterations at a speed like his ≈ 4 h ≈ $3.20 on Hugging Face's L4 ($0.80/h). Not yet measured on any GPU I'd use.
